Technical compliance FAQ

The questions procurement teams ask us.

Straight answers on independent QA/QC, Clerk of Works, snagging and liability — the detail that decides whether a building is fit for its duty.

When should independent QA/QC be appointed?

As early as possible — ideally at design stage, so the inspection and test plan, hold-points and notice periods are agreed before work starts. Most of the decisions that govern quality are made before or during each pour or activity, and concealed work cannot be inspected after the event. A late appointment still adds value, but the independent record then starts on the day of appointment, not at the start of the works.

Which standards do you inspect against?

The project specification and approved drawings first — where they are more onerous than the general standard, they govern. Beneath them sit the recognised standards for the work in hand: BS EN 13670 and BS EN 206 with BS 8500 for concrete, TR34 for industrial ground floors, NHBC Standards for new-build housing, and the Approved Documents supporting the Building Regulations. Every finding we record is tied to the requirement it is measured against.

What does a Clerk of Works do that a site inspector does not?

A site inspection is a point-in-time check; a Clerk of Works is a continuous, client-appointed presence. Our Clerk of Works inspects workmanship and materials against the specification day by day, attends hold-points, and keeps the site diary and photographic record of what was actually built. That continuity is what allows defects to be raised while they can still be corrected cheaply — and what gives the client a contemporaneous record if questions are asked later.

What does a CivPoint snagging inspection include?

A pre-handover or pre-completion inspection of every room and external area, measured against NHBC Standards, the approved drawings and the specification. Each defect is logged with a photograph, precise location and severity rating in a room-by-room schedule that can be issued straight to the contractor. Once remedial works are complete, we re-inspect to verify close-out — so handover is based on evidence, not on a signed-off list.

Who carries the liability for non-compliant work?

Liability depends on where the work departs from its obligations — specification, design or workmanship — and that is precisely what independent evidence establishes. Because CivPoint holds no construction contract and prices no remediation, our diagnosis of root cause is not coloured by an interest in the answer. That neutrality is what makes our reporting defensible in negotiation, expert determination or dispute, and it is the single reason most clients engage an independent consultancy rather than relying on the contractor's own account.

How does independent QA/QC differ from the contractor's own checks?

A contractor's QA confirms its own work to its own programme; independent QA/QC verifies that work against your specification with no stake in passing it. We attend the same pours and apply the same standards, but our records are made for the asset owner — documented hold-points, mix verification and tolerance data that exist independently of the contractor's paperwork. If a dispute arises years later, that independent evidence is what survives scrutiny.
